# game_testing

Modern tech-billionaire mansion game. Current phase: building game-ready assets in Blender (driven over the `blender` MCP server, see `.mcp.json`).

## Asset pipeline
- Every asset is a generator script: `blender/assets/<name>.py`, parameters in a `P` dict at the top, `build(**overrides)` rebuilds its collection from scratch. Run in Blender with
  `ASSET = "<name>"; exec(open("/Users/andrewcarr/Documents/projects/game_testing/blender/run.py").read())`.
  Change the script, not the .blend, so work stays reproducible. The working Blender file is `blender/levels/mansion.blend`.
- Shared helpers: `blender/lib/common.py` (cushions, bent tubes, bevels, box UVs, glTF export), `blender/lib/textures.py` (numpy-generated tileable maps → `assets/textures/`).
- Conventions: metres, origin at floor centre, front faces -Y, root empty named after the asset, parts named `<Asset>_<Part>`. Principled BSDF + image textures only (what glTF carries). UV scale = world scale (wood 0.5 m tile, leather 0.2 m tile).
- Export: `common.export_glb(root, "assets/models/<name>.glb")`. Check in engine: `python3 -m http.server 8765` from the project root, open `http://localhost:8765/viewer/?asset=<name>` (three.js 0.160, same stack as ../video_game).

## Levels (the house)
- `blender/levels/plan.py` is the floor plan as data: rooms (rect, level, kind, double height), OPEN ring corners, CUTS (openings with optional door asset + swing + lock, windows, balustrades, garage door) on wall lines, the floating STAIR, SPAWN. Main house ~52 x 40 m, two floors (ground 0, upper +4.6 m; ceilings 4.2 / 3.8; double-height entry and great room to 8.4 m), courtyard 20 x 14 m ringed by 3.5 m galleries. Future wings attach through the facades (x = ±26, y = ±20).
- `blender/lib/house.py` generates the shell from the plan: per-room floor/ceiling slabs and 0.15 m wall linings (each room owns its side, so finishes are per room), cuts through every lining on a cut's line, glazing, rails with 2.2 m invisible colliders, the stair, then `merge()` joins it into one mesh per (room, material).
- `blender/levels/rooms/<room>.py` dresses a room (interface at the top of `mansion.py`): FINISHES, PLACEMENTS and LIGHTS in room-local metres ((0, 0) = interior centre, z = floor), optional PROBE. `python3 blender/levels/room_info.py <room>` prints a room's interior, doors and windows in those coordinates.
- `blender/levels/mansion.py` builds plan + rooms + props and exports `assets/models/mansion_shell.gltf`, a `.gltf` per prop, `assets/levels/mansion.json` (rooms with probe/rect/floor, props with their room, colliders, lights, spawn). Missing asset modules are skipped (SKIP).
- Build headless (the Blender window is never needed): `Blender -b --factory-startup --python blender/tools/build_level.py -- [--plan] [--room R] [--only-room R] [--no-export --no-save]`; `--plan` renders top-down plans, `--room R` a 4-view interior sheet (`build/previews/`). Without `--no-export --no-save` it writes the shared level and saves `blender/levels/mansion.blend` (what the bake reads).
- Tests read positions from the level file (`viewer/tools/level.mjs`); run them in place after `npm install --no-save playwright-core`.
- Viewer: `viewer/?level=mansion` (click to play; WASD, Shift run, Space jump, C crouch, E interact, I inventory; `&pos=x,y,z&look=x,y,z` sets the eye position/view; `&bake=0` unbaked; `&tm=neutral|agx|aces` tone mapping, default Neutral).
- Jumping: on the press, with a 0.15 s buffer and 0.1 s coyote time; `grounded` is decided once per frame by probing under the feet (set per substep it flickered at run speed and ate jump presses). Mid-air step-ups are limited to 0.1 m.
- Collision: each prop gets one box per mesh part (`common.part_colliders`, skipping trim, wall fixtures and moving parts). Player: `viewer/player.js` (box character, walk 3.4 / run 6.8 m/s, gravity, ~1 m jump, 0.3 m step-up, lands only on what's under it). Stairs also get an invisible ramp along the nosings (`house.stair` → `level.ramps`, followed by `Player.followRamp`), so climbing and descending glide instead of popping 18 cm per tread; `tour.mjs` checks the climb and descent stay on the slope. Stress test: `viewer/tools/walls.mjs` must report 0 impossible positions.
- Crouch is C, not Cmd/Ctrl: Cmd+W / Ctrl+W close the tab and can't be blocked, and macOS drops other keys' keyup while Cmd is held.

## Grounds (outdoors)
- `plan.GROUNDS`: lawn (to x ±90, y ±80), stone terrace ringing the house (to x ±31 / y ±25), perimeter hedge (x ±70, y ±62, drive gap at x 14..25.5 south) and 10 ZONES (rect + triangle budget, ~70k for the estate). Each zone is dressed by `blender/levels/outdoor/<zone>.py` (`PLACEMENTS` in zone-local metres: (0, 0) = rect centre, z = 0 = lawn = world `plan.LAWN_Z` -0.08) with assets `blender/assets/<prefix>_*.py`; trees/shrubs are `plant_*` (planting zone). Briefs: `specs.md` "## Grounds"; agent instructions: `prompts/build_zone.md`; preview: `build_level.py -- --no-export --no-save --zone <zone>`.
- `blender/levels/grounds.py` builds the lawn / terrace / hedge as grids (vertex-lighting resolution), places every zone as world-space copies, splits by material and merges into ~8 meshes (`Mansion_Outdoor_Flat` = every untextured material, one per textured palette material, Water, Glass) -> `assets/models/mansion_outdoor.gltf`. Outdoor materials only from `blender/lib/outdoor.py` (untextured colours are free; textured = stone, teak, foliage, grass, gravel; `water()`, `glass()`).
- Lighting: `bake.py` bakes the outdoor meshes into a "Bake" vertex-colour attribute (flat: colour x light; textured: light) and re-exports the outdoor gltf; `mansion_bake.json` "outdoor" holds the scales. Engine (`viewer/sky.js` `applyOutdoor`): unlit MeshBasicMaterial (vertex colour x scale x texture), no shadows or lights; water is a cheap sky-reflecting shader; lawn gets world-space mowing stripes.
- Walkable (`blender/levels/ground_colliders.py`, from grounds.py): the lawn and terrace are floors, the perimeter hedge a wall with an invisible 6 m boundary just outside it (and across the drive gap behind the gate), invisible walls over the pools and fountain, and every zone's geometry as boxes (~5,100: decks and plinths step up, pergolas walk under, tree trunks' lower 2 m) in `assets/levels/mansion_grounds.json`; the player looks them up through a grid (`?outside=0` leaves them out). Exterior doors in `plan.py` ("exterior doors": the entry's glass pair, dining to the north terrace, the kitchen's glass door, the mudroom's east door; `door_glass*` assets). From outdoors a room's furniture draws only while one of its windows/doorways faces the player (batching.js). `level.grounds` describes the walkable estate (heights, bounds, footprint, doors) for the Grimble; the player is outdoors when `window.__culler.here` is null. Test: `viewer/tools/outside.mjs`.
- Beyond the hedge: `blender/levels/horizon.py` renders the horizon band (tree belts, fields, hills, haze) -> `T_Mansion_Horizon.webp` + `mansion_sky.json`; `viewer/sky.js` draws a procedural sky dome with the band over it (drawn after opaque geometry).

## Scanned assets (Poly Haven, CC0)
- `blender/tools/polyhaven_textures.py` replaces generated texture maps with photo scans (its `MAP`: our map -> Poly Haven id + our tile), keeping each map's name, size, tile and mean colour (the room's palette), so no rebuild or rebake is needed; originals are kept in `build/texture_originals/`, sources listed in `assets/textures/polyhaven.json`. Remove an entry from `MAP` and re-run to restore the generated original. Poly Haven's tile scans run small and rustic: keep large-format, clean surfaces (see the dropped list in the script).
- `blender/tools/polyhaven_models.py` downloads models (glTF, 1k) into `build/polyhaven/models/` for offline renders only (scanned trees are 0.5-4M faces): `horizon.py` instances the scanned trees (falls back to generated ones if they can't be fetched).

## Interactables
- A moving part is its own empty (pivot/origin where it rests) with an `interact` custom property, written to glTF extras (`export_extras=True`) → three.js `userData.interact`, so every placement of the asset gets the behaviour with no level changes. Types: `slide` (drawer: axis, distance), `rotate` (door: axis through the empty's origin, angle), `pickup` (keycard: item, name). Shared fields: duration, range (to the item's nearest edge), prompt/promptActive, side, `collide` (moving collider, solid only once settled), `indicator` (status-light node).
- State lives in the level, not the asset: `PLACEMENTS` options `lock` (item id the interactable needs) and `attach` (parent to another placement's node, e.g. the keycard inside `Desk_Drawer`). A pickup inside another interactable can only be taken while that one is open; inside a hinged container it attaches to the container's static `<Asset>_Inside` node (`inside` = the door's name).
- Locks, keys and caches: `blender/levels/caches.py` is the whole design (chains of 2-3 steps: guest nightstand -> office key -> office door -> desk drawer keycard -> primary suite; library table -> safe key -> office safe -> car key; workbench drawer -> cellar key -> wine room climate cabinet -> locker key -> garage locker -> fuse + flashlight), merged into the rooms by `mansion.py`. Every key opens something; locked things say what they need ("Locked · needs the office key"). Test: `viewer/tools/locks.mjs`.
- Loose things (`blender/lib/pickups.py` has the rule): every hand-sized object standing free on a surface (vases, bowls, cups, glasses, bottles, candles, books lying flat, ornaments, fruit...) is a throwable pickup; the decor kits split their vignettes per item (`pickups.wrap`/`emit`), hand-made assets use `pickups.make`/`adopt`. Shelved rows, built-ins, trays, plants, textiles and floor pieces stay. Resting pickups draw inside their room batch (`batching.js` `unbatch` on focus/take: no extra draw calls); one with others resting on it waits for them. Test: `viewer/tools/pickups.mjs` (census, reach, take, stack, throw).
- Engine: `viewer/interactables.js` (`Interactables`, `Inventory`), `viewer/items.js` (`Hand` shows the armed item, `Throwables` arcs + glass shatter), HUD in index.html. Controls: I inventory (↑/↓ arrows choose, Enter arm), Enter use armed item, Delete/Backspace put away. Keys (keycard) only work armed: Enter at a door with that lock toggles locked. Throwable pickups (`"throwable": true`) are thrown with Enter. Tests: `viewer/tools/drawer.mjs`, `keycard_door.mjs`, `throw.mjs`, `tour.mjs` (the stair: smooth up, down and up again), `movement.mjs`, `inventory.mjs`, `jump_run.mjs` (running + human-length Space taps in four rooms; every tap must jump), `flicker.mjs` (pixels that flip when the camera moves 1 mm in long views: z-fighting shows up as whole surfaces, aliasing as edges; heatmaps in build/flicker/), `walls.mjs` (runs into every wall/corner/door; must report 0 impossible positions). Wave tests: `fuzz.mjs` (random running/jumping/crouching in every room, `SEEDS=7,12`; 0 problems), `controls.mjs` (every wall control: in focus, visible, reachable, toggles), `locks.mjs` (every key has a lock and back; walks a cache chain), `lights_off.mjs` (switches darken rooms with an off bake), `power.mjs` (trips, fuse, flashlight), `outside.mjs` (out the front door, round the house, back in), `sfx.mjs` (every sound fires), `afford_shots.mjs` (highlight/crosshair shots), `hunt_play.mjs` (the Grimble hunt).
- Interactivity v2 (contract: `prompts/interactivity.md`): `viewer/signals.js` (`Signals` = `window.__signals` {get, set, toggle, on(name|'*', fn), define, names}; `power` starts on; `Noise` = `window.__noise` {on(fn), emit({position, loudness})}). Types `switch` (E toggles its `signal`; every switch on a signal follows it; `travel` tips the rocker) and `use` (`needs` item armed + Enter sets `signal` to `value`; `consume`). `react` nodes (object or list: show/hide, emissive, slide/rotate/scale animated, light) follow their signal; light/emissive/visibility reacts are electric (also need `power`; `react.power` overrides); real-time lights come from a pool of 2 point lights (made once, only if the level has `light` reacts) given to the nearest lit reactors. `block_sight` reactors: `interact.sightBlockers()` (Box3s while on), `interact.blocksSight(a, b)`. Placement opts `signal` (string renames the asset's non-power signals, object maps them) and `state` (initial value of the placement's own signal) are written to mansion.json props. `interact.register(root, opts)` adds a runtime-built prop (tests). Initial values: level `signals` > placement `state` > a switch's authored `state` > unset (false; `power` on), whatever the load order. Reactors that only change materials (emissive, show/hide) share one material per (source material, signal, effect) and are batched per room like static props (show/hide = `material.visible`; `?rbatch=0` leaves them unbatched, for comparison); moving ones (slide/rotate/scale, light, block_sight, `collide`) keep their own nodes; a react with `collide: true` gets a moving collider like interact's.
- Items: `flashlight` (armed + Enter toggles one camera SpotLight, 35° / 20 m / no shadow, created at load when the level has a flashlight (adding a light later recompiles every material: ~2 s); 4-min battery, HUD meter; a flat one loads a spare `battery`, an armed battery refills it), `battery`, `fuse`, `car_key`, `key_<id>` (for `use`/`lock`), throwables emit a noise on impact (`noise`, default 1); `shatter: false` ones (books) thud, bounce off walls, land whole and can be taken again. `tablet` (`viewer/tablet.js`, `window.__tablet`): armed + Enter opens the smart-home panel (↑/↓ rows, ←/→ within a row / switch page on the tab row, Enter toggles, I or Esc closes; pauses look/move): Rooms (lights_/blinds_ signals present) and Cameras (one 320x180 feed at ≤5 fps, rendered into a corner of the main canvas before the frame and copied out, culler pointed at the feed camera; cameras from mansion.json `cameras` or 4 defaults); offline while `power` is off. - Affordance (`viewer/affordance.js`, `window.__afford`; contract in `prompts/interactivity.md` "Affordance"): focus only within 50° of the view; crosshair states (take / use / blocked / armed / throw) with the prompt under it; a faint fresnel rim on the focus; brief glints on usable things within ~5 m (one Points draw call, frozen under `__bench`); inventory rows say what each item is for (pickup `purpose`, else a table / derived) with an icon. `?afford=0` off. Shots: `viewer/tools/afford_shots.mjs <out>`.
- `player.knockDown(from)` (`window.__player`): `from` = the attacker's world point (or `{dir}` toward it); shove away (collisions respected), eye drops to 0.3 m and the view rolls ~70°, input ignored 2.5 s, then up; `player.isDown`. - Hiding spots (interact type `hide`, `viewer/hiding.js`, contract in `prompts/interactivity.md`): E climbs into a wardrobe / pantry / closet or under a table / desk / behind a sofa (doors on pivot empties swing open and pull to, leaving a crack; movement locked, view clamped round the spot's facing), E climbs out. `player.hidden` while inside (the Grimble can't see a hidden player), `player.hideSpot` {name, cover closed|open, eye, exit} while in one. ## Baked lighting
- `blender/levels/bake.py` (headless: `Blender -b blender/levels/mansion.blend --python blender/levels/bake.py -- mansion 1024`, ~30 min; lightmaps are denoised with Open Image Denoise) bakes, per room, Cycles diffuse lighting for the shell meshes tagged with that room into `assets/textures/T_Mansion_<Room>_Lightmap.webp` and a 360° panorama from the room's probe (sun hidden, highlights clamped) into `T_Mansion_<Room>_Env.webp` (8-bit sRGB of radiance / 3), bakes the grounds into vertex colours, and writes `assets/levels/mansion_bake.json`. Save the .blend first; re-bake whenever the layout changes.
- The lightmap atlases are generated in `mansion.py` (`common.lightmap_uv`, one 1024 atlas per room); sun, sky, exposure live in its `P`; each room's `LIGHTS` feed the bake: `strip` (glowing ceiling slot, keep for working rooms), `point` (bulb in a fixture prop), `spot` (downlight, picture light, grazer), `area` (hidden cove/backlight/under-cabinet source); see the top of `mansion.py`. Design brief: `prompts/lighting_pass.md`.
- Live fire: a mesh with the custom property `fx = "fire"` (a flat card, see `living_fireplace.flame_card`) is drawn by `viewer/fire.js` with an animated flame shader (additive, no textures, no real-time light; its static glow is in the bake; time freezes under `window.__bench` so shots repeat).
- Engine (`viewer/baked.js`): lightmapped shell materials (cloned per room) take all direct light from the bake (real-time direct light off). Props get diffuse light from their room's order-2 spherical-harmonics probe (`sh` in the bake JSON, computed from the unclamped panorama so ceiling strips light the furniture like the walls) plus the real-time sun, and reflections from the room's highlight-clamped panorama. The bake also denoises lightmaps (mask-aware blur inside UV islands). `?bake=0` shows the old unbaked lighting for comparison.
- Reflections are box-projected: each room's panorama is looked up from the room box (rect/floor/height in mansion.json) so floors and counters reflect walls and windows in place (`?box=0` off); shell reflections are dimmed where the lightmap is dark (`?so=0`); glossy shell materials get uneven sheen (world-space smudge noise + texture grain on roughness, `?sheen=0`).
- Lights off (a light switch darkens its room): `bake.py ... --state off|both` also bakes each room with its own lights off (its `LIGHTS`, ceiling strips and the fixtures' glows on its signal; sun, sky and the neighbours' lights stay on) into `T_Mansion_<Room>_LightmapOff.webp` / `_EnvOff.webp` (q90, dim: ~0.1-0.55x the on maps' size) plus `rooms[r].off` in the bake JSON (signal, off SH, tint). A room's signal is `mansion.json rooms[r].lights` (`LIGHTS_SIGNAL` in its module, else the signal most of its fixtures are wired to: `lights_<room>`, `lights_galleries` for the 8 galleries, `lights_courtyard` for the festoons); a LIGHTS entry with `signal=None` is never switched (the courtyard's candles). Engine (`baked.js`): each room fades (0.45 s) to dark while its signal is false or `power` is off: the off lightmap is packed into the on lightmap's alpha as a luminance ratio at load (no extra GPU texture; off = on x ratio x mix(tint, 1, ratio)), the props' SH is lerped in place, reflections are re-filtered (PMREM) from a blend of the two panoramas a few times per fade, the room's ceiling strips fade with it. `window.__lighting.lightLevel(room)` (1 = lit, down to the dark state's share) / `isDark(room)` for gameplay; `?loff=0` ignores the off bakes; test: `viewer/tools/lights_off.mjs` (walks to each switch, presses E, measures the frame). Approximations: a room's dark state keeps its neighbours' light through the doors (so power off is slightly brighter than truly dark at open-plan edges), and a TV or fire baked into a room stays in both states.
- Rugs (any asset with "rug" in its name, `mansion.baked_prop`) are copied into the shell at build time and lightmapped like the floor, so furniture's contact shadows land on them; their textures still shrink to 512 (optimize_textures).
- Screen effects (`viewer/fx.js`, on by default): GTAO contact shadows at quarter resolution (normals from the main pass's depth, no second geometry pass), a half-resolution bloom on HDR highlights, tone mapping + SMAA (a multisampled half-float target cost more than all effects). Quality levels 2 (AO + glow) / 1 (glow) / 0 (none, direct render) / -1 (none, 1x pixel ratio); a governor steps down while < 50 fps over 2 s windows; no real GPU (SwiftShader/llvmpipe) = off. `?fx=0` off, `?fx=1` force on, `?q=N` fix the level. M4 Pro 1600x900@2: ~2.0 ms off, ~2.8 glow, ~5.2 all.

- Time of day: `mansion.py` `LOOKS` ("day", "golden"; `LOOK` selects) holds the sun direction/strength/colour, the bake's sky light, the sky dome's horizon/zenith/haze and the ceiling-strip colour; it's written to `mansion.json` ("sky", the sun light) and read by `horizon.py` and the engine. Changing it needs rebuild + full bake + horizon render. The build also recomputes which rooms get direct sun (`sunlight.py`).
- Furniture shading: `lib/vertex_ao.py` bakes each asset's own ambient occlusion into an "AO" vertex colour at export (only faces under 0.25 m²; big panels would smear), cached in `build/ao_cache/` by geometry hash; three.js multiplies it into the base colour.

### When to bake (rules)
- Don't bake after every change. Only changes to light need a bake: the shell (walls, windows, openings), ceiling strips and lamp lights, sun/sky, or new geometry that casts shadows or bounces light (furniture placement counts only for the room it's in; the grounds for the outdoor bake). Props' look, gameplay, UI and engine changes never need one.
- While iterating, use the cheapest bake that answers the question: the unbaked view (`&bake=0`) for layout; `--outdoor-only` (~10 s) for grounds changes; `--rooms R,...` for the rooms that changed only; or a draft bake at 64 samples (~8 min for the whole house). A room's lights-off state alone: `--state off --rooms R` (~40 s a room at 64 samples, against its stored on state); an on-only re-bake keeps the room's old off state (with a WARN: re-bake it if the room's light changed).
- The full bake (`-- mansion 1024 --state both`: on + lights off, ~60 min; on alone ~30) runs once before committing or pushing, or when the user is away. Batch changes into one full bake; ask before starting one while the user is waiting.

## Web performance budget (it ships as an online three.js game)
- Baseline (2026-10-17, `viewer/tools/budgets.json`): 13.3 MB download (433 files, Draco geometry, WebP), ~276 draw calls mean / 657 peak (frames that redraw the shadow map), 501k triangles peak, ~210 MB GPU textures incl. lightmaps and env maps; M4 Pro at 2400x1350: ~550 fps, GPU 1.3 ms p50 / 2.1 ms p95; SwiftShader (no GPU) at 1280x720: ~4 fps.
- What keeps it there (all automatic): identical prop materials from different files are folded into one at load (`shareMaterial` in index.html: same name, colours, maps, flags), so reusing a material across assets is free; `viewer/batching.js` then merges each room's static, opaque props into one mesh per material and `RoomCuller` skips furniture in rooms > 18 m away (shell, galleries and courtyard always draw); shell merged per room+material (`house.merge`); `common.export_gltf` Draco-compresses geometry; `blender/tools/optimize_textures.py` (run by build_level) shrinks prop textures to 512 (shell textures stay 1024); lightmaps 512 except the big volumes (1024).
- Never add real-time lights per room in the engine: one RectAreaLight per ceiling strip took the house to 3 fps. All interior light is baked.
- With the screen effects the GPU timer query over-reads (12.8 ms reported for 5.2 ms frames): frameMs is the gate in budgets.json, gpuMs is informational; triangles/draw calls count every pass.
- Measure: `viewer/tools/perf.mjs <url>` (unique download, draw calls, texture MB) and `viewer/tools/fps.mjs <url> metal|swiftshader W H DPR` for quick checks.

## Quality loop (better looks at the same cost)
The goal is more realism without more compute: `budgets.json` is a cost ceiling (the baseline plus a noise allowance), and the screenshots are what improves. The user judges looks.
- `node viewer/tools/bench.mjs <label>`: a scripted run at run speed through the house (`bench_route.mjs`: entry, galleries, great room, dining, kitchen, stair, upper galleries, office; ~53 s, uncapped frames) on two profiles, `gpu` (Metal, 1600x900 DPR 2) and `soft` (SwiftShader 1280x720, a stand-in for weak hardware). It records per-frame frame/GPU (timer query)/CPU time, draw calls and triangles, plus load time, download and texture MB, and per-room p95. Output goes to `build/bench/<label>/stats-<profile>.json`, a PASS/FAIL per budget and a line in `bench_history.jsonl`.
- `node viewer/tools/shots.mjs <label>`: 32 fixed views (a wide view per room and hero-prop close-ups), HUD hidden. They are deterministic (0% diff between identical runs).
- `BENCH_QS="&fx=0"` (bench and shots) appends to the page URL for A/B runs; `node viewer/tools/grid.mjs out.png <cols> a.png b.png ...` makes a labelled contact sheet.
- `node viewer/tools/compare.mjs baseline <label>`: a metrics table against the budgets, plus per-view pixel diffs, written to `build/bench/<label>/compare.html` (space = before/after, D = diff heatmap, arrows = views).
- Loop: make one change, then run shots, bench and compare, then show the user compare.html. Keep the change only if no budget is OVER and the user likes it; otherwise revert it. The deterministic counts and SwiftShader timings are the real gate; M4 timings are within ~5-10% noise. Re-baseline (and update budgets.json) only on purpose.
- Offline work is free by construction (bake samples/denoise, texture generation at the same resolution, geometry at the same triangle count); runtime changes must pass the budgets.

## Building assets in parallel
- Machine limits: every headless build takes a run slot (`blender/tools/slots.py`): at most 2 house builds (`build_level.py`, ~2 GB + GPU each) and 4 asset builds at once, the rest wait. Agents must still run one Blender command at a time: 7 agents each previewing every room in parallel (~38 Blenders) once ran this 26 GB machine out of memory.

- Spec: `specs.md` "### grimble"; concept and the AI source sculpts (TRELLIS.2 / Hunyuan3D on Hugging Face, from `concept/grimble/front.png`) in `concept/grimble/` (README there). - Inside (`viewer/grimble_hunt.js`, `grimble_nav.js`): after `loopsBeforeSmash` rounds at the glass it plays Smash, the great room's west glass bays shatter (`plan.CUTS` `breakable=(i, j)` -> their own `Mansion_Breakable_*` meshes, listed in `level.breakables`; `shatter()`), it keeps its staff (dragged in the right hand), and it crawls (scaled to 0.68 by the crawl clips: 2.55 m wide, 2.8 m tall, so it fits the 3.2 m galleries and the 3.0 m courtyard door with room to turn; `grimble_qa` ENVELOPE) over a nav grid of the main spaces (great room, entry, galleries, courtyard, kitchen, dining; side rooms are unreachable: their doors are too narrow; after the break-in the grid is flood-filled from it, so it only plans for places it can reach). - Senses (it only knows what it perceives; `hunt.lookTarget` aims its eyes): sight = a ray from its head stopped by the shell (not glass: house.py tags glazing `glass`, older levels by the 0.12 m boxes; not rails), shut doors (interact rotate items' colliders), closed blinds, tall furniture, and, for a crouched player, low cover close in front of them; a forward cone (`fov` 1.45 rad half) plus `closeSense` 3.5 m all round; a notice meter `aware` (~0.9 s at 10 m, x distance; slower crouched / still / dark: `__house.lightAt`, else `__lighting.isDark`, else lights_ signals; faster running, flashlight on, or already alert). `player.hidden` can't be seen (it sniffs at a hiding spot it saw them enter). Hearing: `window.__noise` leads {pos, time, loudness} (20 s), heard along the walkable route or muffled x0.6 per wall, newer/louder replaces; in a chase a fresh noise moves `lastSeen`; loud or near ones it rushes to.
