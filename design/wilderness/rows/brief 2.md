# Section 14: Frost Night in the Bloom

**Hook.** Past the gate there is no wilderness. There is the billionaire's square mile of almond
trees in full bloom on the coldest night of February: 20,000 identical trees in exact rows, frost
fans roaring on their towers, petals coming down like snow, and a pink glasshouse glow on the fog
to the south. Every row is a 1.5 km rifle lane. Step one row sideways and you are gone.

## Why it isn't the modal answer
The stock answer is a forest, a river and mountains; the stock *farm* horror is the cornfield
(*Signs*, *Children of the Corn*). This is neither. It is the land a tech billionaire actually
owns around a house like this (Gates is the largest private farmland owner in the US; the Resnicks
own the Central Valley's almonds and pistachios): flat, laser-levelled, irrigated, automated, and
almost empty of people. Nothing is wild; everything is in a grid. The horror is repetition and
geometry, not darkness: a grid of trees is a perfect hiding place and a perfect shooting gallery
at the same time, depending on which way you face. The ground is pancake-flat (the valley floor
falls about 1 m per km), which also subverts "epic terrain" and is cheap to build.

The place is real: a PLSS section (one square mile, 1,609 m) in Tulare County, California, bounded
by section-line roads, like every square mile around it to the horizon. The estate sits on the
north edge of its own section.

## Goal and arc (12-18 min)
**Goal: reach the freight siding at the packing house in the section's south-east corner and get
on the slow unit train that comes through at dawn.** The train is the clock; the frost fans are
the cover; dawn is the deadline.

1. **Night (fans on).** About -1 °C, clear, ground fog (Tule fog) 0.5-3 m deep in the low spots,
   visibility 120-180 m. The frost fans in every orchard block run, a deep two-blade roar that masks
   footsteps within ~120 m of each tower (the hearing system's noise floor). Petals fall.
2. **Grey (5:40).** The first horn, far east. Fog thins; the eastern sky goes ash-pink over the
   Sierra. The fans are still on.
3. **Dawn (6:20).** The fans spin down block by block, a wave of silence crossing the valley; the
   fog lifts to 600 m+ and every row becomes visible end to end, for them too. The train crawls
   through the siding at ~12 km/h for 90 s. On it = escape. Missing it = hunted in daylight (very
   hard, by design, like staying in the house past the 50).

Arriving early is also bad: you wait in the most watched corner of the map.

## Layout (plan metres: x east, y north, estate origin; gate x 14..25.5 at y -62)
Playable: the section south of the hedge plus the flanks, **x -790..820, y -1530..-62 (~1.6 x 1.5
km)**. Blocks are 400 x 400 m, separated by dirt headlands (E-W, at y -480 and -900) and farm
avenues (N-S, at x -390 and 420).

- **The Drive** (x 14..25.5, y -62..-1530). The estate's cypress avenue stops dead at the gate;
  outside it is a plain 7 m farm road running dead straight 1.5 km to the south county road. The
  invaders' vehicles come up it. The most obvious path is the worst one.
- **Bloom blocks E1/E2/W-flank** (x 30..420, y -80..-900 and the flanks east and west of the
  hedge). Mature almonds, 6.7 m between rows x 5.5 m in the row, **rows N-S**, canopy 1.6-5.5 m,
  trunks clear below. Along-row sightline: the whole block (400 m, 800 m where blocks align).
  Across-row: two rows standing; crouched, you see (and are seen) 40-60 m under the canopy skirts.
  Diagonal aisles exist too: a grid has more than two lanes. The safe spot is off every lane,
  tight to a trunk.
- **E3 (x 420..820, y -80..-480): the quincunx block.** An older block planted in a hexagonal
  pattern: three lane directions at 60°. It disorients on purpose.
- **E4 (x 420..820, y -480..-900): the pull.** A 25-year-old block being removed: half the trees
  ripped out and stacked into 3 m root-ball windrows (whole-orchard recycling). Tall, ragged cover
  in lines, open dirt between. The only place with real cover *and* long views.
- **The Holding Yard** (x -770..-30, y -100..-560): the container-tree nursery the estate's garden
  was bought from. Frost-cloth block (white fleece shrouds over hooped olives and citrus, rows of
  human-height white shapes); the specimen block (boxed Italian cypress 3-9 m in ranks; the
  estate's olive allée has its replacements here); 30 black shade-cloth hoop tunnels 9 x 120 m
  (dark, blind, two exits each); clipped topiary cones and balls identical to the west garden's.
- **The windbreak** (y -900, full width): a line of blue gum eucalyptus 25-30 m tall (the tallest
  thing in the section, visible from everywhere) over a concrete irrigation lateral 3 m wide x 2 m
  deep. **Chokepoint**: crossings only at x -390, 20 and 420 (bridged headland crossings, watched),
  plus a 0.9 m pipe culvert at x 230 you crawl through (crouch).
- **The Shop Yard** (x 30..160, y -930..-1040): equipment barn, fuel tanks, parked autonomous
  tractors (no drivers' seats), tree shakers, two farm UTVs (car_key), the frost-fan control
  panel and the farm's camera hub.
- **The Pivot** (centre -380, -1180, radius 260 m): a circle of winter rye in a 1 m swale where
  the fog pools deepest. Open killing ground with one moving thing: the 260 m pivot arm, creeping
  round at ~3 m/min at its end tower, its sprinklers on. Below freezing the spray glazes the truss
  and the rye with ice.
- **The Glass** (x 100..460, y -1120..-1400): a 10 ha high-wire tomato glasshouse, 7 m gutters,
  aisles 1.6 x 280 m, grow lights on all night. Its glow turns the fog pink for kilometres (the
  real Leamington, Ontario "pink sky"), so south is always findable. Pipe-rail heating trolleys in
  the aisles. Packing hall on its east side (x 460..560).
- **SE young block** (x 460..820, y -900..-1450): 3-year almonds, 3 m tall. Cover for crouching
  only.
- **The Siding** (x 560..800, y -1450..-1500): loading docks, a parked labour bus, the spur off
  the mainline. **Final chokepoint**: every route converges here.

**Edges** (all dangerous, none are invisible walls): west, the concrete-lined canal at x -830
(25 m wide, slick 1:1.5 sides, fenced; real canals drown people), behind the canal-side
eucalyptus; south, the county road (y -1530) + rail embankment (y -1560, +1.5 m); east, the county
road at x 820 with patrols, and beyond it the neighbour's dead section (orchard ripped out, dust,
root windrows); north, the estate. Beyond all of it, the same grid continues into fog: an
unwalkable backdrop of orchards, dead sections, power pylons, a town water tower with a red light
far south-east, the Sierra at dawn.

## Routes (gate → siding)
- **A. Down the Row** (orchard, ~1.6 km, 4-6 min). South through E1/E2 inside the N-S rows,
  across two headlands, through the windbreak (culvert at x 230 or the x 420 bridge), down the
  young block. Fastest, best noise cover (fans), worst sightlines: one guard at a headland
  crossing watches four lanes.
- **B. The Holding Yard** (nursery + pivot + glass, ~2.3 km, 8-12 min). West through the shrouds
  and the hoop tunnels, cross the windbreak at x -390, then the pivot (inside its spray curtain, or
  around the fogged rim), into the glasshouse's west door, east down the aisles to the packing
  hall. Slowest, most hidden, the most places to be ambushed at close range.
- **C. The Drive edge + UTV** (~1.6 km, 3-8 min). Hug the first row beside the drive, take the
  shop yard (steal a UTV: fastest, loud, headlights you can't turn off), or go on foot into the
  glasshouse's north door and ride a trolley 280 m south. The gamble route.

## Pursuers and the Grimble
- **Foot squads** follow from the gate (north) and sweep south in a line abreast across rows.
- **Vehicles** (pickups, UTVs with roof spotlights) come up the drive from the county road and
  patrol the headlands. As one rolls along a headland, its spotlight flashes down each row mouth in
  turn: tick, tick, tick.
- **Riflemen** climb the frost-fan towers (ladders to a 10 m gearbox platform): above the
  canopy they see the white sea, not you, but they see where trees shake.
- They use the land like you do: they post at row crossings (four lanes per guard), hold the
  windbreak bridges, and at dawn the whole orchard becomes theirs.
- **The Grimble (easter egg)**: if the player dawdles (no progress for ~4 min) or misses the train,
  it comes over the horizon from the west. At 7.3 m, its head and shoulders clear the 5.5 m canopy:
  a black shape moving over a white sea, with petals cascading in its wake. In the specimen
  block it can stand still among the 8-9 m cypresses; one of them isn't a tree.

## Player verbs and systems
Existing: run/crouch/jump, throw (a lure), noise (`window.__noise`), sight blockers, switches and
signals, tablet cameras (the farm's camera masts), car_key, flashlight (suicidal in a row; useful
in a hoop tunnel), knockDown, the Grimble hunt AI. New: **shake a tree** (E: a petal fall plus
noise; a decoy), **petal tell** (any body brushing a canopy drops petals: you can read pursuers
by it, and they read you), **noise masking field** (frost fans raise the hearing threshold per
block; the fan panel switch kills a block's fans: silence for them and for you), **row-aware
line of sight** (the orchard is a lattice, so sight is computed arithmetically against the
lattice: no raycasts against 20,000 trunks), **moving platforms** (pipe-rail trolley, the train),
**vehicle patrols** on the headland graph with fake spotlight cones, **ice** (a slippery patch
under the pivot), a world clock (fog, fans, horn, train).

## Signature moments
1. **The gate.** You slip out; the road runs 1.5 km dead straight; at its far end, headlights turn
   in: the next wave is coming up the drive toward you. Petals drift across the road in the gate
   lights. You step into the bloom.
2. **The strobe.** A pickup idles down the headland and its spotlight flashes down each row in
   sequence, closer, closer; you have one beat to get behind a trunk before your row lights up.
3. **It snows three rows over.** You haven't touched anything.
4. **The pivot.** Crossing 500 m of open, fog-filled rye inside the moving spray curtain, ice
   ticking on the truss, keeping pace with a machine that walks at 3 m/min.
5. **The dawn wave.** The fans stop one by one across the valley, east to west; in the silence
   the fog lifts and you can suddenly see 800 m down your row. So can the man at the end of it.
   Then the horn.

## Look and sound
Night: blue-black sky, no moon; almond blossom near-white with a pink cast; trunks black; fog
lit pink-orange from the south by the glasshouse and cold blue elsewhere; the eucalyptus line a
black wall; sodium-orange shop-yard lights; the vehicles' white spotlights. Dawn: ash-pink and
grey, long cold shadows. The house phase is golden hour; the invasion runs through the night;
the escape is the frost night into dawn (a new `LOOKS` entry: "frost_night" and "dawn"). Sound:
fan roar as a positional bed per tower (the map's real soundscape; you navigate by it, and a
broken, silent fan is a landmark), bee hum near the hive pallets, the eucalyptus creaking, the
glasshouse's ventilation fans and drip irrigation clicks, the far freight horn, the pivot's
motor clunk, gunfire flattened and echoless (no hills).

## Production and browser cost
**Cheap**: flat terrain (one low-res heightfield per 200 m chunk; ditches, swale, embankments as
meshes); fog (it is the draw distance at night: 150 m); all repetition (one InstancedMesh per
LOD per species per 200 m chunk); frost-fan blades (rotation in the vertex shader); petals (one
GPU particle system around the player, plus burst emitters); the grid's analytic sight; baked
vertex light (moon/sky + a pink fill from the south) as the grounds do today; unlit
MeshBasicMaterial outdoors.
**Expensive / to watch**: tree count (~21k mature + 5k young + ~30k nursery pots): near LOD
(~800 tris) only within 40 m, crossed cards to 150 m, one strip impostor per row beyond (the row
is a texture: a row seen end-on is a few quads); **dawn** (fog lifts, view distance x4: the
impostor rows must carry it); the glasshouse (no real glass: frosted emissive panels, interior
draws only when inside or within 60 m); spotlight cones (additive cards, never lights, within the
2-point-light pool only for the player's flashlight); the train (~40 hoppers, one instanced mesh).
Target: < 350k tris, < 200 draw calls, < 12 MB download for the level.
**Assets** (`wild_*` generators): almond tree (3 ages x 3 LODs + row impostor), frost fan tower,
hive pallet, root-ball windrow, eucalyptus windbreak card tree, nursery set (boxes/pots, cypress
column, boxed olive, topiary cone/ball, frost-cloth shroud, hoop tunnel), irrigation riser and
valve box, concrete lateral + culvert, center pivot tower + span, Venlo glasshouse bay (4 x 8 m
repeat) + tomato row card + pipe-rail trolley, packing hall shell, labour bus, pickup + UTV,
autonomous tractor, tree shaker, fuel tank, camera mast, rail track + hopper + locomotive, canal
section, dirt headland. Horizon band re-render (`horizon.py`) with orchard rows and fog, so the
house's windows already show the bloom before you ever go out.

## Risks
- **Monotony** is the point but can stop being fun: the identity of each block (age, pattern,
  pull, nursery) and the navigation cues (pink south, eucalyptus wall, fan sounds, the drive)
  must carry it. Playtest getting lost on purpose vs by accident.
- **Long rifle lanes** can feel unfair: fog at night, invaders need lit/clear LOS for a hit,
  telegraphed spotlights; dawn is meant to be lethal.
- **Dawn cost**: the view distance jump; impostors must hold the frame budget.
- **Reads as "a farm level"** rather than horror: keep it night, keep the shrouds, the empty labour
  bus, the driverless tractors, the dead section across the road (the billionaire's water: green
  inside, ripped-out orchards outside).
- **Seasonal truth**: bloom, frost fans, bees and Tule fog are all February; no corn, no
  sunflowers, no harvest machinery running.
