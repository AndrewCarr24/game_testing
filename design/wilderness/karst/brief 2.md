# Hollow Ground (wilderness concept, slug `karst`)

**Hook:** beyond the gate is a bare limestone plateau with no rivers and no trees, where every
piece of cover, and every way down, is a hole.

## Why it isn't the modal answer
The stock wilderness is a forest with a river and mountains. Hollow Ground turns each part of that
inside out. There is **no forest**: trees grow only down in sinkholes, so from a distance the land
looks bald. There is **no surface river**: the one stream on the plateau falls into a cliff and runs
under your feet. There is **no mountain**: the relief is negative (pits, slots, a 110 m shaft), the
tallest thing is a man-made fire tower, and the land ends in a cliff edge, not a peak.
The cave isn't the whole level either. It is one of three routes, and it isn't a fantasy grotto:
it's a show cave the billionaire bought and closed, with his data centre built into one of its
chambers. The estate is the strange thing in this landscape: an irrigated green lawn, a lap pool and a
reflecting pool on rock that can't hold water. The players can work that out with no text.

The place is a southern-European *causse*, the high limestone plateaus above the Tarn gorges.
That fits the estate's olive, lavender and box palette, because the garrigue outside the hedge is
the wild version of the planting inside it. Patches of the plateau are limestone pavement
(*lapiaz*), dissolved out along the rock's joints. The Burren is the visual reference.

## Goal and arc (a proposal)
**Get off the plateau.** The valley floor is 170 m below the south edge: a village, a river bridge
and a church you can see from the gate. A run ends when you cross the bridge (180, -1330).
It takes 8-20 min. The arc has three acts:
1. **Exposure** (0-3 min): bright, flat, open ground, with guns on the road behind and ahead.
   You pick a route by what you can see.
2. **Below** (3-12 min): the cave, the grykes or the sinkholes. You stay hidden by going down. A
   storm comes in from the west on a visible clock.
3. **The edge** (last 2-5 min): the descent is the climax. Every route has to get down a cliff.

## Layout (plan metres: +x east, +y north, estate centred at 0,0; see map.svg)
- **Walkable:** x -700..720, y -40..-1180 (1.4 x 1.15 km), plus a finish strip on the valley
  floor. The plateau sits at z 0 (the lawn) and rises to +12 at the tower knoll. The moor in the
  west rises to +60 as backdrop, behind the billionaire's 2.4 m deer fence at x -700.
- **Edges:** south, the scarp, a 150-170 m cliff you can only get down at the three descents; east,
  a side gorge at x ~720; west, the deer fence and the moor; north, the estate (nothing on the
  plateau beyond it is walkable).
- **The apron** (gate to y -200): the lawn stops dead at the hedge. Outside it, the drive turns
  into a single-lane road over grey rock. Two invader vans are parked at (30, -210).
- **The Clints** (limestone pavement), x -650..-30, y -100..-820:
  - Clints are slabs 1-3 m across. Grykes are the joints between them: 0.3-0.9 m wide and 1-2 m
    deep, running roughly N-S and E-W.
  - The wide grykes are a natural trench network, crouch-walkable.
  - Some clints rock and clonk when you step on them, which makes noise.
- **The Fire Tower** (-280, -560): an 18 m steel lookout on the knoll, with a rifleman in it. From
  there he covers most of the Clints out to about 400 m.
- **The Dolmen** (-430, -760): a stone portal tomb, which reads as a silhouette against the sky.
- **The Sotchs** (doline field), x 60..660, y -100..-760:
  - About 40 bowls, 20-90 m across and 4-25 m deep.
  - Each has a red terra-rossa floor (sometimes a field) and is the only place trees grow (downy
    oak, box). Tree crowns poke up out of the holes.
  - Dry-stone sheepfold huts (*cazelles*), each a hiding place with one door.
  - A dew pond (*lavogne*) at (250, -300).
- **The Blue Doline** (430, -470): a collapse doline 45 m across with sheer 22 m walls down to
  turquoise water. The billionaire put in a steel spiral stair and a floating dock.
- **The Swallet** (-280, -280): the moor stream comes east down a blind valley 8 m deep and drops
  25 m into a cave mouth. A dye-tracing rig stands at the lip.
- **The Hole** (-60, -610): a collapse shaft 70 m wide and 110 m deep, which is the skylight of the
  cave's great chamber. It has a black steel fence and a cantilevered viewing platform.
- **The Vent** (110, -780), by the road: the data hall's concrete head-house, a transformer yard,
  and a lift plus stair going 60 m down. A plume of steam (warm cave air) stands 60 m tall.
- **The Turlough** (-360, -900): a lake that comes and goes, of the *polje* kind (cf. Lake
  Cerknica). It is a flat meadow 420 x 220 m and 6 m below the pavement. A black moss
  "bathtub line" runs round its rocks and walls. *Estavelles* in its floor drain it in dry weather
  and spout water in the storm.
- **The Lip** (-170, -1040): a dry valley runs south from the turlough to the top of a curved
  cliff, 80 m high, where a waterfall used to fall. A via ferrata of iron rungs goes down its face,
  then a scree path drops the last 90 m.
- **The Grimble's trail:**
  - About 120 fresh collapse holes, 2-4 m across, one every ~9 m, alternating 3 m either side of
    a straight line.
  - The line runs from a raw climb scar in the cliff at (-554, -1060) to the estate's west hedge
    at (-70, -10).
  - It crosses the cave at (-250, -400), where one step broke through the roof.
- **The cave** (~1.0 km, from z -25 down to -170). Each stretch is listed with its length:
  1. Swallet → streamway → **Grimble Hole** (-250, -400), 124 m: daylight and a soil cone you can
     climb out on.
  2. → **the Duck** (-170, -500), 128 m: a low wet crawl, and the part that floods.
  3. → **the Ballroom** (-60, -610), 155 m: a chamber 250 x 120 m and 120 m high, under the Hole.
     The river roars in a gorge, and the show cave's iron footbridge crosses it 45 m above the water.
  4. → **the Data Hall** (40, -780), 197 m: a white, cooled room of server racks in a chamber,
     reached through the show cave's concrete tunnel, with the shaft up to the Vent.
  5. → the lower streamway → **the Rising** (60, -1190), 410 m: a 6 m flooded duck you swim
     through, coming out in a green pool at the foot of the cliff. Next to it are a closed ticket
     kiosk and a car park.

### Routes (gate to bridge)
| Route | Length | Time | Cover | Threat | Descent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Road** | ~1.45 km | 3.5 min run | crash barrier, walls, the Vent's steam | vans at 150 m, checkpoint (170,-930), riflemen shooting down the hairpins | 5 hairpins |
| **2. Over the Top** (Clints or Sotchs) | ~1.6 km | 8-12 min | downward: grykes, bowls, cazelles, fog | the tower's sector, kill bowls (a doline found from its rim is a trap), the Grimble | the Lip via ferrata (exposed), or the climb scar |
| **3. Underground** | ~1.6 km (1.0 under) | 10-15 min | complete from guns | dark (flashlight, 4-min battery), flood clock, followers with torches | the Rising swim |

The routes are linked, which is what makes the choice real. Halfway along each route there is a
way across to another:
- The Grimble Hole joins the Clints to the cave.
- The Hole and the Vent shaft go down from the surface into the Ballroom and the Data Hall.
- The Blue Doline is the Sotchs' hub, with an optional sump into the cave.

Possible extra (orchestrator's call): the armory's back wall could be a natural rift with a keyed
steel bulkhead. Behind it, the billionaire's own bolt-hole runs to the Data Hall: a shortcut on a
second run.

**Chokepoints:** the gate; the vans; the checkpoint and the hairpins; the walled drove lane between
the Clints and the Turlough at (-150, -820); the Duck; the Ballroom bridge; the Data Hall bulkhead
(keycard); the via ferrata; the Rising swim; the bridge (all three routes end there).

**Landmarks visible from far off:** the Vent's steam column, the Fire Tower, the church in the
valley (the goal), the Blue Doline's ring of trees, the Dolmen, and the Hole's black platform. All
of them are vertical shapes on a flat plain, so on a clear day you can always see where you are.

## Pursuers and the Grimble
- **Invaders:**
  - Whoever is left of the house waves follows through the gate.
  - The van crew is 4 with one rifle; the rifleman stands on a van roof.
  - The tower spotter doesn't move. His binoculars glint (a sprite) before he fires.
  - The checkpoint has 3 people and a vehicle with emissive headlights.
  - Guns make the sightlines across the plain deadly. Only three things beat them: going down
    (grykes, bowls), walls, and the fog.
  - If they see you go in, 2-3 follow you into the cave. Their torches are lit from the existing
    2-point-light pool, or are fake cones, and you can see them coming a long way down the passage.
- **Insect silence:** the cicadas stop within ~30 m of any invader. It works as a free detector and
  needs no HUD.
- **The Grimble:** its trail is there in every run, telling the player something big walked here.
  If the player summoned it in the house (the easter egg), it is also out on the plateau.
  - It strides over the pavement and looks down into the grykes, so a gryke hides you from men
    but not from it.
  - Only roofs hide you from it: the cazelles, the cave, the fog.
  - It's too big for the cave, but it reaches down through the Hole and the Grimble Hole (a
    reversed ReachUp).
  - Underground, its footfalls are the existing camera shudder plus dust falling from the roof.

## Verbs and systems
**Reused:**
- walk, run, crouch, jump; hiding spots; `knockDown`; the grimble_hunt senses.
- Flashlight + battery is the cave's resource gate. Spares are in a cavers' cache in the Ballroom
  and in the Data Hall store.
- Throwables + noise: a stone dropped down a gryke draws a guard, and the cave echoes.
- Locks/keycards: the Data Hall bulkhead, the gatehouse key.
- `power`: the Vent yard feeds the house, so a blackout in the house also kills the Data Hall.
- `sightBlockers`: the steam plume and fog banks.
- Tablet cameras: the billionaire's aquifer and wildlife cams show the Duck's water level and the
  checkpoint.

**New:** terrain chunks with LOD and streaming; analytic pavement collision; mantle/climb out
(ledges of 1-1.5 m); ladder/rung climbing; wading (slow and loud); a short breath-hold swim (2
places); an animated water level (the flood); weather (fog density feeds the AI's sight range, plus
rain); fall death; invader gun AI with line of sight against a heightfield; a horizon render of the
valley and gorge, replacing the tree belts in `horizon.py`.

**The storm clock:** rain curtains are visible over the western moor from the gate. They arrive
about 6 min after you leave.
- The Swallet stream turns brown, and the Duck sumps at about +10 min. Anyone still upstream is
  shut in with whoever followed them, and has to get out by the Grimble Hole.
- The estavelles spout and the turlough fills, so crossing it becomes wading.
- Fog rolls up the scarp and cuts sightlines from ~400 m to ~60 m.
- So the storm hurts cave runners and saves surface runners: time decides which route is right.

## Signature moments
1. **The line of holes.** You notice that the sinkholes run in a dead-straight line and are fresh.
   You follow it to the cliff edge and find the scar where something climbed 150 m up out of the
   valley.
2. **Boots overhead.** You lie in a gryke while a rifleman steps from clint to clint above you, his
   soles 40 cm from your face. Then the cicadas start again.
3. **The bridge in the dark.** You cross the show cave's iron bridge 45 m above a river your
   flashlight can't reach. A disc of daylight hangs 110 m up, and something blots it out.
4. **A server room in a cave.** You wade out of black water into a white, humming, air-conditioned
   hall with a thousand blue LEDs. Then the house loses power and it all goes dark.
5. **The Rising.** You hold your breath under a rock lip and come up in a fluorescent green pool
   at the foot of the cliff. The village bell is ringing, and invaders' torches move along the
   edge above you.

**The first 30 s:** the slatted gate slides open. Ten metres on, the green stops as if cut with a
knife. Grey rock stretches flat in every direction and glares under a low gold sun, and every joint
in it is a black line. The fountains' sound is gone and the wind takes over.
- On your left, tree crowns stick up out of holes in the ground.
- On your right, a steel tower stands on a rise.
- Ahead, about 1 km away, the land just stops. There is haze where it ends, and a church spire far
  below.
- A white plume of steam rises beside the road.
- 150 m down the road, a man stands on a van roof with a rifle, facing the other way.

## Look
- **Palette:** bone limestone #d8d2c4, gryke black #1d1b1a, terra rossa #9a3b24, box and juniper
  green #3d4a2c, straw grass #c9b27a, orange lichen #d98a2b, fluorescein green #3cff6a (in the
  dye-traced water only), Data Hall white #f2f4f6 with blue #3aa0ff LEDs.
- **Light:** the existing "golden" look, with the sun ~19° up from just south of west. The raking
  sun is what draws the grykes and half-shadows the dolines. The storm dims it: two baked vertex
  sets (sun and overcast) blend in the shader.
- **Sound:** wind, cicadas, sheep bells from the valley; the clonk of loose clints; the Swallet's
  roar and the cave's drips and echo; the Data Hall's hum, which carries up the Vent and serves as
  a sound beacon; thunder walking in from the west; the church bell (the goal).

## Production and browser cost
**Cheap:**
- The plateau is a heightfield (64 m chunks, 3 LODs, the existing outdoor vertex-colour bake). It is
  the cheapest possible wilderness: almost no foliage cards, no canopy, no overdraw.
- Dolines are bowls in the heightfield.
- Fog and rain are fog density plus a camera-attached particle box.
- The cave is a spline tube generator (like `common` bent tubes), ~35k tris, plus ~20k for the
  Ballroom. It is enclosed and dark, lit by the flashlight, and culled as rooms with the surface off
  while underground.
- The Data Hall is an interior built with the house pipeline (shell + lightmap) and emissive LEDs.
- The steam is 6-8 sprite cards.
- One crater mesh plus a decal, instanced ~120 times for the trail.
- Instanced dry-stone wall modules.

**Moderate:**
- The near-field pavement: 8 authored 16 x 16 m clint tiles (~1.5k tris), instanced and rotated
  within 60 m, with a gryke-line texture beyond. Collision comes from each tile's 0.25 m height grid,
  with no per-clint colliders.
- The cliffs, using Poly Haven rock scans.
- The Ballroom's scale, hidden by darkness.

**Expensive:**
- the flood and swimming
- gun AI with line of sight over 1.4 km (heightfield raymarch plus a coarse occluder grid of walls
  and dolines)
- terrain streaming
- distance impostors for 1 km views

**Estimates:**
- Surface: ~150-200k tris and 40-80 draws, under the 501k / 657 peak.
- Download: ~+8-10 MB of Draco chunks and six 1024 WebP textures (limestone, gryke, terra rossa,
  grass, scree, cliff). It streams while the player is still in the house.

**Asset list:** clint tiles x8, rubble, erratics; dry-stone walls, cazelle, lavogne, dolmen; fire
tower; dye rig; aquifer well-heads (they blink, and mark the cave's line on the surface); Swallet
mouth; cave tube generator, flowstone instances; show-cave rails, concrete path, iron bridge; Data
Hall shell, racks, cooling pipes; Vent head-house, transformer yard; road, hairpins, crash barrier;
invader vans, checkpoint; via ferrata rungs + cable; Rising pool + kiosk; Grimble crater; valley
backdrop (horizon render).

## Risks
- The gryke maze could be tedious or disorienting. Keep the grykes short, have some open out into
  bowls, and keep the tower in view as a compass.
- Rifles on a flat plain can feel unfair. Telegraph them (glint, insect silence) and give the
  player fog and down-cover.
- The cave could play as a corridor. The three links and the flood loop are there to stop that.
- The player controller has a 0.3 m step-up limit, so grykes would trap legs without a mantle verb.
- Tiling of the pavement tiles may show from the tower, so the far field needs a texture with no
  obvious repeat.
- Swimming and the flood together are a big new system. Fallback: the Duck closes with a scripted
  roar and a rising decal, and the Rising becomes a wade.
- Mixing places could make it incoherent. Keep it one causse with patches of pavement.
