# The Negative

**Hook:** the house was cut out of this hole. Past the gate is the abandoned limestone quarry that
supplied the estate's stone. Its benches are 7 m tall, which is exactly one Grimble.

## Why it isn't the modal answer
- It isn't a forest, a river or mountains. The land is made by people: a stepped bowl 640 x 460 m
  across and 70 m deep, cut in terraces that look like an amphitheatre. Its parts have real names
  (benches, haul roads, a crusher, a conveyor, a loadout). It reads more like architecture than
  nature.
- It is the estate's opposite. The house is pale limestone, rectilinear and immaculate, and the
  quarry is the same stone left raw and abandoned. In one corner the billionaire's builders opened
  a fresh dimension-stone cut five years ago: wire-sawn walls, razor-straight rooms, and the void
  the great room's stone came out of. Robert Smithson called places like this "ruins in reverse"
  (the hole a building leaves behind). Rachel Whiteread's *House* is the inverse.
- The setting is coastal California, not the generic temperate north. Think Permanente Quarry in
  Cupertino, the cement pit next door to Apple Park and the Los Altos Hills estates. The ground is
  gold dry grass, invasive pampas grass and fennel on the spoil, with blue-grey eucalyptus. A
  marine-layer fog pours over the ridge at dusk.
- The water is wrong. The pit lake is a Caribbean turquoise because lime and cement-kiln dust push
  it to pH 11 (Harpur Hill's "Blue Lagoon" in Buxton is pH 11.3, and the council dyed it black to
  stop people swimming). It looks inviting, and it burns.

## Goal and arc (8-15 min)
**Goal: reach the old rail loadout on the south rim and drop onto the stone train.** The working
cement plant beyond the ridge still runs unit trains along the quarry's line. They crawl up the
2% grade under the dead loadout gantry at about 1.5 m/s, one every ~3 min. Real loadouts load
trains that are moving at walking pace, so the boarding is believable.
1. **0:00 Gate → rim (200 m).** The estate's clipped green stops at the hedge. Outside: a cattle
   grid, a crushed-limestone road, gold grass on old spoil mounds, the weighbridge and scale house.
   To the east, the invaders' cars are parked across the road with their doors open and hazard
   lights on (emissive). The road crests, and the pit opens at your feet (moment 1).
2. **0:30 Choose a route** (below). The whole level is visible from the rim, including the
   pursuers.
3. **2-10 Descent and crossing.** The fog rises in the west lobe, invaders come down after you,
   and a vehicle group sets off round the rim road to cut you off at the loadout.
4. **8-14 Climb out, board the train.** Miss the train and you have to hide for 3 minutes.
   **Ending:** the train rolls into a rock cutting and the pit drops out of view.

## Layout (world metres: x east, y north, estate centre 0,0; gate at 20, -62; see map.svg)
- **Apron** (y -62..-210): capped spoil, dry grass, fennel. Estate road to the county road east
  (x +600, where the invaders come from). Weighbridge **W** (60, -150). Rim lookout **R**
  (30, -210, z 0).
- **Pit:** rim x -320..320, y -220..-680. Ten 7 m benches with 8 m catch benches (real bench
  geometry), floor at z -70. The lobes:
  - **East lobe (old cement pit):** rough blasted faces, rockfall netting draped down the walls,
    coyote brush and toyon on the benches.
  - **Pit lake:** centre (100, -470), 260 x 170 m, surface at -64. The two lowest benches show
    through the water as pale steps sinking into blue-black.
  - **Silt flats** (-50..30, -500..-575): grey settling-pond crust.
  - **West lobe, "the Cut"** (-320..-90, -250..-560): fresh dimension stone in sawn rooms and
    slots 1.2-12 m wide, stepping down in 7 m lifts linked by steel stairs and ladders. On its
    floor, the **block yard**: 2-3 m rejected blocks stacked in a maze, with spray-painted codes
    (paint marks only, no words).
- **Crusher C** (190, -215): a 28 m rust-and-cement tower on the north rim. **The Spine:** the
  overland conveyor runs from C to the loadout, 515 m long on 12 lattice towers. The pit grew under
  it after it was built (real stranded infrastructure), so it now hangs 75 m above the lake at
  mid-span.
- **Loadout L** (120, -735): three 30 m concrete silos with a slow red beacon, a gantry over the
  track, and a fenced yard. **Rail** runs east-west at y -760 in a cutting.
- **Edges:** north is the estate hedge. East is the county road (blocked: invader vehicles keep
  arriving). West is a 35° chaparral and eucalyptus ridge with fog spilling over it. South is the
  rail cutting, then a scrub hill. All of these are unwalkable backdrop. **Playable area: ~900 x
  740 m.** Horizon beyond.

### Routes (R → L)
| Route | Length | Character | Trade |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Haul Road** (east) | ~2.1 km | 12 m wide pale road spiralling down the east wall at 8-12%, with 1.2 m safety berms. Then the **Causeway** (an old haul embankment, 170 m across the lake) and switchbacks up the south wall | Fastest to run, but open to every rifle on the rim. Crouching behind the berm is your only cover. Vehicles use it too |
| **The Cut** (west) | ~1.2 km | Sawn rooms, slots, ladders, then the block yard, then the "drill stair" (steel stairs bolted up the south benches) | Safe from guns, dark and foggy. Close-quarters invaders with bats and knives, dead-end slots. The Grimble can't fit in slots under 2 m |
| **The Spine** (centre) | ~0.7 km | Walk the conveyor gallery from the crusher, above the lake. Rusted cladding, missing deck plates (running jumps of 1.5-4 m), one span with its roof gone | Shortest, but one-way, with only two tower ladders as exits. Steel underfoot doubles your footstep noise. Your silhouette shows through holes in the cladding. **Option:** restart it from the crusher's switch room (power signal). The pre-start horn (real conveyor practice) wakes the whole pit, then you ride the belt at 3 m/s |

**Chokepoints:** the Causeway, the Spine's mid-span, the drill stair, and the loadout gantry
ladder. **Long sightlines:** the rim sees the floor at 300-400 m. Rule: no point on the floor is
more than 25 m from cover (blocks, berms, boulders, trestle legs, fog).

## Pursuers and the Grimble
- **Invaders** follow on foot from the gate and come down the Haul Road and into the Cut. Riflemen
  take the rim (the crusher top and the lookout). Their "torches" are emissive cones with sight
  cones, not lights. **The race round the rim:** a vehicle group drives the 1.6 km rim road to
  the loadout, and you can watch their headlights crawl round the bowl. That is the clock.
- **The pit carries sound.** Noise inside the bowl reaches the rim at 1.5x loudness, with a
  0.4 s echo. Loose rock falls on its own now and then. These are false noise events that
  invaders investigate, and so can you (throw stones: the floor is an unlimited supply of
  `shatter: false` throwables).
- **The Grimble** (easter egg: only if you lingered in the house) comes over the west ridge through
  the fog. **It is one bench tall.** It treats the benches as a staircase and takes the direct line
  down the wall while you use the switchbacks. Its eyes just clear each lip. It can't enter slots
  under 2 m wide (it reaches in), and it sinks to the waist in the silt and wallows for 10 s,
  where you only slow to a wade. It can reach the Spine near the ends. At mid-span it uses its
  Knock clip on a tower leg, and the whole gallery shudders (the existing camera-shudder system).

## Player verbs and systems
**Existing:** run, crouch, jump, noise and throwables, flashlight (the slots, the crusher interior
and the dusk), hiding spots (block voids, the crusher hopper, under the wrecked 777 haul truck,
whose tyres are 2.7 m tall), switches and power (conveyor restart), lock and key (the shot-firer's
key), knockDown, the sight and hearing AI.
**New:** large terrain chunks and streaming; ladders (a climb verb); fall damage (a 7 m drop
kills, and bench edges have berms so they read clearly); slow surfaces (silt) and damage water
(caustic); moving supports (belt, train wagon: a moving version of `followRamp`); height fog; one
scripted destruction (the blast, swapped in like the breakable glass); AI vehicles on splines; a
coarse nav grid for 1 km.

## Signature moments
1. **The Reveal.** 30 s past the gate the road crests and the pit drops 70 m at your feet: a white
   stepped bowl, the turquoise lake, the rusted Spine across the sky, the silo beacon on the far
   side, and headlights already moving along the rim.
2. **Ruins in reverse.** Deep in the Cut you step into a sawn void exactly the size of the great
   room (8.4 m tall), with the house's 1:1 facade mock-up standing in it (high-end builds order
   full-size stone mock-ups). On the floor lies the cracked twin of the gate pier you just walked
   through.
3. **One bench tall.** On a lower bench, the Grimble's hood rises over the lip above you. It walks
   along beside you, looking down, then steps down to your level like it's taking a stair.
4. **Follow the wire.** Take the shot-firer's key from the magazine bunker on the rim and follow
   the orange firing cable down to the concrete blast hut on the floor. Firing it sounds three
   siren wails (a pit-wide lure), then drops the face over the Causeway ramp, cutting the vehicles
   and everyone behind you off.
5. **The stone train.** From the gantry you drop 3 m into a moving wagon of crushed stone. The
   train's roar masks every sound. Behind you, the fog fills the bowl to the brim.

## Look and sound
**Time:** golden hour fading into blue dusk over the run. The pale limestone holds the last
light, so the pit glows lighter than the land around it, the reverse of a dark-pit cliché. The sun
sets behind the west ridge, so the east wall turns gold-white while the west lobe sits in shadow
and fog. **Palette:** chalk white, bone and warm grey stone, dry gold grass, pampas plumes lit from
behind, blue-grey eucalyptus, rust-orange steel, cement grey, and the single saturated note:
alkaline turquoise. **Weather:** the marine layer pours over the ridge and rises in the pit from
z -70 to about -35 over the run (one uniform: a readable second clock). **Sound:** wind across
the bowl, sheet steel ticking as it cools, trickles of scree, ravens echoing, distant train horns
(the schedule you can hear), engines on the rim, your footsteps changing from gravel to sawn
stone to steel plate.

## Production plan and browser cost
**Cheap:** the benches are offset outlines extruded into ribbons (vertical face plus flat tread).
That is huge scale for very few triangles, it box-UVs with two limestone textures (weathered,
sawn), and it vertex-bakes well because every face has its own normal. Blocks, boulders, grass,
pampas and fennel are instanced. The water reuses the existing shader, tinted. The fog is the
existing fog plus a height term. The reveal works from one rim panorama.
**Moderate:** the conveyor kit (1 truss segment and 1 tower, instanced), the crusher (hero, ~10k
tris), silos and gantry, the train (loco plus one wagon instanced x30 moving on a line: 2 draw
calls), vehicles on splines.
**Expensive:** the whole pit is in view from the rim, so culling barely helps and the triangle
budget is global (aim ~280k visible: terrain 60k, Cut 25k, blocks 36k, vegetation 80k, Spine
30k, crusher and loadout 25k, train 20k). Also AI and nav over 1 km, train boarding physics, the
blast swap, and baking vertex light over 1 km (bench toes need AO). Target: +6-8 MB, about 8
textures at 1k, about 80 draw calls.
**Assets:** `quarry_bench` (terrain generator in lib), `quarry_cut` kit (rooms, slots, steel
stair, ladder), `quarry_block` (3 sizes plus the cracked pier), `quarry_boulder` x4,
`quarry_crusher`, `quarry_conveyor_span` and `quarry_conveyor_tower`, `quarry_loadout` (silos,
gantry), `quarry_weighbridge`, `quarry_magazine`, `quarry_blast_hut`, `quarry_net` (rockfall
drape), `quarry_haul_truck` (wreck), `quarry_mockup` (reuses the house materials), rail kit,
`train_loco`, `train_hopper`, `plant_pampas`, `plant_fennel`, `plant_eucalyptus`,
`plant_coyote_brush`. Also re-render the house's horizon band so the crusher top and the silo
beacon show on the south skyline as foreshadowing.

## Risks
- **Guns versus open ground:** a bowl favours snipers. Cap effective range around 150 m with
  spread, so 400 m shots only pin you down.
- **Falls frustrate:** every lethal edge needs a berm or a bright sawn lip, plenty of ladders, and
  clear ramp reads.
- **The "house negative" story must read without text.** The mock-up, the pier twin and the
  great-room void all carry it. Test whether players notice.
- **The Spine is one-way:** keep the two exit ladders, and think about motion sickness on the belt
  ride.
- **Draw distance:** the whole pit is visible at once. Use impostor vegetation beyond 150 m and
  fog as the LOD hider.
- **Missing the train** is a soft fail (3 min wait). Tune it so it's tension, not tedium.
