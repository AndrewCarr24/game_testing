# The Offset (slug `tidal`)

**Hook:** the billionaire drowned his own salt works to sell the marsh as carbon credits, and
now the tide he let back in is the clock, the trench, the road out and the wall behind you.

## Why it isn't the modal answer
No forest, no river, no mountains, no dark woods to hide in. The estate's knoll is an island in
the baylands south of it: a dead-flat industrial landscape of old salt evaporation ponds (pink,
rust, olive, chalk white), levees laid out like a circuit board, pylons standing in the water, a
white mountain of harvested salt, a sinking stilt-cabin hamlet, a drowned orchard and a 570 m
shell pier out to a screw-pile lighthouse. Flat land is the opposite of horror cover: you can be
seen from a kilometre away. Cover comes from **timing**: the creeks are trenches only while the
water is out, the fog comes in only with the flood, the road exists only at low water. The
billionaire's greenwash project ("managed realignment", levees breached on purpose; *blue
carbon* is real, saleable marsh carbon) is the level, and it tells his story with no text.
It's a real kind of place. Its model is the South San Francisco Bay salt ponds (Cargill's
Newark salt stacks, the Don Edwards refuge, the breached Alviso ponds) and Drawbridge, the
ghost town sinking into the marsh 10 km from Silicon Valley. That makes it believable next to a
tech fortune, and it matches the estate's Pacific-coast planting.

## Goal and the run's arc (5-12 min)
Reach the mainland (south sea wall or the east rail station), or reach the boat moored at the
lighthouse and take it out to sea. One global **tide** h(t) runs a compressed semi-diurnal cycle:
low water -1.8 m, high water +1.8 m (MSL datum), 24 min per full cycle. It starts with the first
invader wave, so *how long you stayed in the house decides what the marsh looks like when you
get out*. Escape early and the flats are open but the flood is coming. Escape late and the land
routes are gone, the boat floats and the fog is in. Max rise 0.47 m/min; on the 1:500 outer
flats the waterline advances at about 3.9 m/s, faster than you can walk (3.4) and slower than
you can run (6.8). That's the real "faster than a galloping horse" tide of Morecambe Bay, cut
down to game size.
1. **The reveal** (0-30 s): out of the gate, down the cut lane, the bay opens below you.
2. **The choice** at the Gatehouse junction: a tide board shows the water, and the three ways
   split.
3. **The crossing** (3-9 min): the water rises behind you, beside you and ahead of you.
4. **The cut**: the route closes; one last chokepoint (the ford, the swing bridge, the pier
   gap, the creek mouth).
5. **Out**: you climb the sea wall into the sodium light and roar of the freeway frontage, or
   the boat lifts off the mud into fog.

## Layout (world metres, estate frame: x east, y north; gate at x 14..25.5, y -62)
Playable box x -950..+780, y -60..-1260 (about 1.7 x 1.2 km). Backdrop beyond it: open bay to
the west and north, far-shore hills, a long bridge in haze, and the freeway on its causeway to
the south as a band of moving lights.
- **The Knoll** (estate island, +7 m at the hedge, shore at +2 about 180-230 m out). Cypress lane
  from the gate to the **Gatehouse** (20, -240): 170 m in a 2 m deep cut (cover), the island's
  only road junction. The invaders' vans park here.
- **The Salt Ponds** (y -250..-700, x -350..+650): a 200 x 230 m grid of ponds between levees
  (crest +3.0, 6 m wide, riprap sides). *Managed* ponds hold still brine at their own level (they
  don't tide: mirror-flat pink and rust squares). *Breached* ponds (A1, A3, A4, B5) fill and
  drain through 25-40 m levee gaps (chokepoints: waist deep at mid tide, with current).
- **The Drowned Orchard** (in A1/A3, x -350..-150, y -260..-690): about 600 dead apricot trees in
  perfect 7 m rows (the Santa Clara Valley was orchards before it was chips), standing in the tide.
  The rows are sniper lanes along their length and cover across them. A farmhouse on a mound
  (-250, -470) has five tide lines on its siding.
- **Mud Slough** (main channel, 30-80 m wide): from the bay mouth (-950, -700) east across the
  map to the **East Slough** (x about 680). Bed -3.5 m, so it's a 3-4 m deep mud gutter at low
  water and a river at high. Crossings: the **Ford** on the causeway; the **footbridge** at the
  ghost town (deck +1.0, awash from h +0.9); the **riffle** at (-150, -820), which you can wade
  only below h -1.0; the **swing bridge** on the east trestle.
- **Pickle Island** (ghost town, about (-420, -860)): 14 stilt cabins of an old duck-hunting
  club, tilted at different angles as they sink, linked by boardwalks, with a water tower (+18,
  a landmark). Hiding spots, a sink-box blind, a bridge-tender-style key on a nail.
- **The Outer Marsh** (y -880..-1200): pickleweed plain at +1.2 (red-violet), cut by a
  branching network of creeks 1.5-3 m deep. The low route runs through it. **Pump House**
  (-350, -1230) on the sea wall: its sluice gate works with the signals system.
- **The West Flats** (x < -350): bare mud sloping 1:500 west to the bay channel, with flocks of
  shorebirds and **oyster racks** (about 200 rack-and-bag tables, 0.7 m tall, in rows) near the
  mouth. The **Shell Pier** runs 570 m from the knoll's west shore (-230, -90) to the **Light**
  (-640, -480), a screw-pile lighthouse on the edge of the bay channel (gallery +15). Deck +4. Bays 38-40 are gone: at h < -0.5
  you climb down a ladder, cross 30 m of mud and climb back up. The boat lies on the mud by the
  Light and floats at h > +0.8.
- **Salt Works** (east): yard and conveyor around the **Salt Mountain** (560, -390), a 25 m
  white cone. It's the high ground: it looks over the whole map, and invader riflemen go up it.
  Climbing it is loud (salt crunch).
- **The Trestle** (rail): crosses from the yard over the East Slough on a swing bridge (680,
  -520) to the **East Station** (780, -570, exit). The bridge sits open for barges. A switch in
  the tender's hut closes it in 40 s. Invaders can open it again.
- **Pylon line**: eight 40 m lattice towers on caissons, 250 m apart, from (700, -150) to
  (-800, -1250). Landmarks you can steer by in fog, and the wires crackle louder as humidity
  rises.
- **Flux towers**: 3 thin carbon-monitoring masts (solar panel, blinking red light, camera) in
  the breached ponds. Their cameras are feeds on the tablet.
- **Edges**: the sea wall (+4) along y -1230 (south exit) and the East Station. Water deeper
  than 2 m everywhere else: swimming drains a stamina meter (the battery-meter UI) and the
  current drags you back. Beyond that is backdrop.

The routes cross each other (the Ford sees Pickle Island; the Mountain sees everything), so you
can switch routes when the tide turns against you.

## Pursuers and the Grimble
Invaders follow you out of the gate and also come *in* from the mainland. At low water they
drive 4x4s along the causeway and onto the flats, headlights strung across the mud. At high
water they come in RIBs with searchlights up the slough and over the flooded ponds (the light is
an additive cone mesh plus a sight check, not a real light). Riflemen take the high ground: the
Mountain, the levee crests, the Trestle. **Fog** comes in from the west with the flood and
scales every sight range (about 600 m clear, 40 m in the bank), so the land trades one danger for
the other. The AI's navigation grid stores each cell's lowest height and is walkable only while
h + 0.3 is below it, so the tide reroutes the invaders too. They take the levees and avoid soft
mud. A chaser who follows you onto the soft flat (glossy black patches) sinks to the knees and
the water comes for him. No gore.
**The Grimble** (easter egg, as in the house): if it came for you in the house, it follows you
out. Out here it's the only thing the tide can't stop: 4 m of water is waist deep on it, and it
sees over every levee. The creeks still hide you, and the soft flats bog it down to the thighs.
At high water in the fog it comes in "over the horizon" from the bay, walking along the channel
markers.

## Player verbs and systems
**Reused:** walk/run/crouch/jump; E interact; signals (the sluice gate floods the pond behind
you; the swing bridge; the Light's lamp, whose sweep you can kill with `power`); noise (mud
squelch, reed rustle, salt crunch, splashes, birds; throw a shell or bottle into the reeds as a
lure); hiding (cabins, the sink box, under the boardwalks); keys (bridge hut, `boat_key`);
flashlight; tablet (flux-tower and tide-board cameras, plus a tide-curve page, which is a UI
graph, not lore); knockdown; the Grimble's senses and search.
**New:** the global tide uniform and one water plane; depth-based wading (speed x (1 - depth/1.4),
noise up), a swim mode with stamina and current; a soft-mud mask (slow, sticky, keeps footprints
for a while, which trackers can follow); heightfield ground for the player and colliders
(today's player knows only boxes and ramps); tide-aware nav; height fog; birds as an alarm (a
running player flushes a flock 60 m away, and the flock is a sight event the invaders notice);
vehicles and boats for the invaders.

## Signature moments
1. **The road goes under.** You run the Ford as sheets of water slide over the asphalt from both
   sides. A van that tried before you sits in the water, its headlights still on under the
   surface. You climb the refuge box on its stilts while the pursuers' headlights stop at the
   water's edge behind you.
2. **The trench.** At low water you walk 2 m below the marsh in a mud gutter while torches pass
   along the levee above. At the creek mouth ahead the water starts creeping up the bed toward
   you, and you have to climb out into the open.
3. **Holding the Light.** You crossed the pier gap at low water. Now you hold the lighthouse
   gallery while invaders come 500 m down the pier in single file through the fog, and below
   you the boat creaks and lifts off the mud.
4. **The marker that moved.** A line of channel stakes stands in the fog. One is taller. It
   steps.
5. **Pull the sluice.** At the Pump House you open the tide gate, and the pond your pursuers are
   crossing turns into a moving sheet of water with their flashlights bobbing on it. Then ten
   thousand dunlin lift off the flats at once and give away where you are.

## Look and sound
**Palette:** salt-pond pink-magenta (halophile algae), rust, olive, chalk-white crystallizer
crust; red-violet pickleweed, tan cordgrass; grey-brown mud that turns into a sky-coloured mirror
when wet; bleached timber; oxide-red pylons; the Mountain's white. **Light:** the existing
`golden` LOOK going on dusk. The fog bank stands as a wall to the west, gold on top, blue
underneath, and rolls in with the flood. The Light's beam turns every 10 s (an additive mesh; it
lights the fog, not the ground, and it raises the invaders' notice of anything it sweeps). The
freeway's sodium strip on the south horizon; the estate glowing on the knoll behind you.
**Sound:** the flood's hiss (a noise bed scaled by dh/dt, the player's real clock), mud
ticking and popping as it drains, curlew and dunlin calls, a foghorn every 30 s from the bay,
power lines crackling in the damp, the far freeway, the boat's hull knocking once it floats.

## Production plan and browser cost
Separate level file (`wilderness.json`), streamed after the gate.
- **Cheap:** one water plane at height h(t) with the existing sky-reflection water shader, plus a
  height texture so the shader knows the depth (shallow colour, foam band). Managed ponds are
  fixed planes with flat colour. The terrain is a numpy heightfield (the levee grid is just
  rectilinear rules; creeks are a noise-steered tree carved in) in 64 m chunks with 3 LODs
  (2/4/8 m grid; creek detail in a 2k height and normal map). Vertex-colour bake as in the
  grounds. Pond and pickleweed colours are vertex colours (free). Wetness comes from z vs h(t) and
  from "time since last covered" in the shader. Repeated structures are instanced modules: pylon
  x8, trestle bay x60, pier bay x75, oyster rack x200, dead tree x600 (4 variants), cabin x14
  (4 variants), marker stake x80. Birds are one Points mesh on the GPU. Fog is FogExp2 plus a
  height term.
- **Expensive:** tall reeds (alpha overdraw, so they stay at the creek edges only); the
  heightfield player and colliders and the tide-aware nav (new engine work); vehicles and RIBs;
  invaders at 300-600 m (they need cheap LODs); a tidal bore up the slough (stretch).
- **Targets:** at most 250 draw calls, 600k triangles peak, 15 MB download, 120 MB textures (5
  tileable terrain maps at 1024: mud, salt crust, pickleweed, levee gravel, riprap; one horizon
  band).
- **Assets (`marsh_` prefix):** levee, breach, riprap; pier bay, broken bay, ladder; the Light;
  boat; trestle bay, swing bridge, tender hut; pylon; salt mountain, conveyor, yard sheds; stilt
  cabin x4, boardwalk, water tower, sink box; farmhouse, dead apricot x4; oyster rack; flux tower;
  tide board; refuge box; pump house and sluice; channel stake; drowned van; grass cards x3.
- Re-render the horizon from the house: the south and west views become bay and fog instead of
  fields.

## Risks
Flat can read as empty (mitigation: the grid, the pond colours, the landmarks, fog layers). Long
sightlines plus guns can feel unfair (mitigation: fog, creeks, the beam's rhythm, a long reaction
window at range). Players may not read the tide (mitigation: hiss, tide boards with red bands,
the wet line, birds moving landward, the refuge box's ladder). A single flat water plane is a
simplification: real estuaries lag and slope, but the channels still fill first because they're
lowest. Drowning can feel cheap (stamina plus a gasp warning, and the current pushes you back
before it kills you). The engine work (heightfield ground, swimming, nav) is the real cost.
