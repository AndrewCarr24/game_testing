# Drawdown

**Hook:** past the gate the lake he bought the view of is gone. Between you and the way out is
a kilometre of open mud, and the only cover is the town they drowned to fill it.

## Why it isn't the modal answer
The stock wilderness is a forest, and a forest hides you everywhere. A drained reservoir is the
opposite: a bowl 1.3 km across, no trees, watched from a rim road where men with rifles can see
all of it. Cover only exists where people built something: the drowned town's foundations, a
church spire, the old road's embankment, a grid of dead orchard trunks, the river's re-cut
channel, the billionaire's own water pipe. You hide in the ruins of the infrastructure.

What you walk on is a mechanic too. Soft silt keeps your footprints and the invaders track them.
Dead clam shells crunch loudly. Dry cracked crust is quiet and firm. Water wipes your tracks but
slows you down. The dam isn't a set piece to blow up: it's the way out, over it or through its
plumbing.

It's a California foothill reservoir in deep drought, which fits the estate's planting. The
irrigated lawn stops in a hard line at the gate; past it the grass is dead gold. A newly
extended steel pipe runs from the estate's pump house to the last pool, and its pump barge is
the only machine still running in the valley. Nobody has to say whose drought it is.

## Goal and the arc of a run
Reach the canyon road below the dam (map EXIT, ~1.7 km south of the gate). From the gate you see
the whole problem: the dam at the far end, two intake towers, a concrete "chalice" spillway on a
spur, and headlights moving down the east rim toward the dam.

**Out the gate (0:00).** The gate is where the invaders came in. When you reach it from the
pool-house armory, most of them are inside the house: on the drive sit their empty trucks and
one or two watchers. The drive drops 80 m to the shore road. On the knoll beside it is the
drowned town's **relocated cemetery**, headstones moved up the hill in the 1950s, all facing the
water that isn't there. The stones are blank (no text in the game). They're the first cover.

**The race (0:00-4:00).** When you pass the gate, a truck leaves the convoy for the dam: ~1.8 km
of rim road at ~10 m/s plus ~45 s to react, so it's on the crest at about 3:45. The Old Road to
the crest is ~1,610 m: 237 s (3:57) at a full 6.8 m/s run. On a pure sprint you lose by ~12 s,
so the crest is taken unless you buy time (the bell) or fight for it with armory weapons. The
chalice is never closed, but it's slow and leads into the dark. Later waves (the 20, the 50)
arrive as more trucks on the county road from the east: the longer you stayed in the house, the
more lights on the rim.

## Layout (estate plan metres: x east, y north; gate at x 14..25.5, y -62)
Heights: lawn z 0 · rim and shore roads -5..-10 · **old full-pool line -18** (top of the white
bathtub ring) · terraces -18..-40 · flats -40..-46 · **water -52** · dam crest -14 · dam toe and
powerhouse -90 · backdrop hills +60..+200.

- **The bathtub** (y -150..-350, all round): 25-35 m of slope banded by a white mineral ring,
  stepped into wave-cut terraces, one per year the water dropped. The terrace lips hide you from
  people below, never from the rim.
- **The flats** (y -350..-900): cracked red-brown crust, grey soft silt in the low spots, shell
  beds, old field fence lines, telephone poles, a **dead orchard** on a 12 m grid (x 150..300,
  y -420..-540) and a **stump field** (y -820..-940). Head-high willow and tamarisk on the delta:
  all two dry years have grown back.
- **The drowned town** (centred x 70, y -650; ~250 x 200 m): the main street is the old county
  road. Roofless foundations, a gas-station canopy frame, a school gym shell and a **brick church
  with a broken spire** (26 m; its tip at -19, just under full pool, so it showed in every dry
  year). A **stone arch bridge** takes the west street over the channel. South of the town **the
  road runs into the water**; the rest of the town shows as dark roofs just below the surface.
- **The pool** (y -950..-1250): deep water wall to wall in front of the dam. **Two intake
  towers** stand 38 m out of it, bridged to the crest.
- **The chalice** (x -305, y -1140): a morning-glory spillway, a funnel 22 m across, its lip at
  -18, standing 22 m tall on a dry rock spur, with a footbridge from the west rim road. A door at
  the foot of the stem opens into the bottom of the drop shaft; a 350 m outlet tunnel slopes down
  (-40 to -85) through the west abutment to a portal below the dam.
- **The dam**: a concrete arch with a 380 m crest road under sodium lamps; downstream, the 76 m
  face, the powerhouse, pylons climbing the south ridge, and the canyon road out.
- **Edges**: north, the estate hedge. East and west, 30 m bathtub cliffs under the rim roads,
  fenced hills above. South, the dam and the canyon walls. NW, the inflow arm narrows into silt
  cliffs. Inside the bowl **deep water is an edge** (no swimming in v1); the shallow margin is
  slow and splashes (noise). Playable ~1.3 x 1.75 km; hills and canyon beyond are backdrop.

## Routes
| | **A. Old Road** (centre) | **B. The Channel** (west) | **C. Under the Rim** (east) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Path | shore road, switchback cut down the bathtub, the causeway (2 m embankment), the town, the road into the water; along the pool's north shore to the haul road and up to the crest | down the west terraces into the river's re-cut channel (a 3-8 m trench in silt), under the town bridge to the pool, the stump field to the chalice | the estate pump house, following the pipe past the foot of the boat ramp (extended four times, four colours of concrete, ending 350 m short of water), the stranded marina, the foot of the east cliffs on the pipe, the pump barge, the haul road |
| Length | ~1,610 m to the crest (3:57 running) | ~1,750 m to the chalice, silt (2.5 m/s) and crouching (~9-12 min), then the 350 m tunnel | ~1,490 m to the crest, pipe and talus at walk pace (~7 min) |
| Exit | crest (a race, then a fight) | chalice tunnel (always open, dark) | crest east end, from below and behind the trucks |
| Protects from | nothing, except the dust fronts | every rim sightline | the rim riflemen: **dead ground** under their road |
| Costs | exposed to all three overlooks | slow; silt keeps your tracks; banks slump; you can't see out, so you go by sound | close quarters: trucks and voices 30 m above; invaders stop at the marina and barge |
| Systems | Noise (the bell), sight range vs dust | footprint tracking, hearing, crouch | sight blockers (cliff edge), noise, armory weapons |

**Chokepoints:** the single-lane switchback cut (seen from the rim); the stone bridge (every
west-east channel crossing); the road's end; the chalice footbridge and stem door; the crest
(380 m of straight parapet).

## Pursuers and the Grimble
- **Followers** (4-8 on foot): out of the gate ~45 s after you. They follow your footprints in
  silt and lose the trail on crust, shells, concrete and water.
- **Rim riflemen**: trucks stop at overlooks O1-O3, each covering a 600 m sector. A scope glint
  and a near-miss in the mud come before a hit. In dust, sight drops to ~40 m.
- **The dam party**: the 3:45 truck, 3-4 men holding the crest's east end, headlights visible
  from anywhere in the bowl.
- **The Grimble** (easter egg, only if you stayed in the house long enough): it comes over the
  dam, a silhouette on the crest scaled by the 38 m towers, then wades the pool thigh-deep and
  patrols the waterline and flats. Too big for the tunnel (4 m bore) or the trench floor, it can
  still **step over the channel** you're crouched in.

## Player verbs and systems
Reused: walk, run, crouch, jump, the flashlight (the tunnel, ~3 min of battery), throwables as
lures, Noise, sight cones and notice meters (`grimble_hunt.js`), locks, power, armory weapons.
New:
1. **Surface types** on the terrain (speed, footstep noise, tracks): crust, silt, shells, water,
   rock.
2. **Footprint decals**: a ring buffer of ~256 instanced quads that the followers' search reads.
3. **Dust fronts**: every ~2.5 min the west wind lifts a tan wall off the flats; for 40-60 s the
   fog closes in and AI sight range shrinks.
4. **The church bell**: a `switch` making a loudness-8 noise heard across the bowl. The trucks
   and the Grimble turn toward the town; it buys ~40 s, enough to win the crest race by ~28 s.
5. **Outdoor AI**: sight across the terrain (heightfield ray tests), trucks on splines.

## Signature moments
1. **The knoll** (first 30 s): behind you sprinklers tick on a lawn that ends at the gate. In
   front, blank headstones and the whole emptied bowl: the white ring, the spire standing in the
   mud, telephone poles marching out to a band of brown water, the dam, headlights already moving.
2. **The bell.** You haul the rope in the roofless church and every light on the rim swings
   toward you.
3. **The dust crossing.** You sprint the causeway as a dust front comes in, and the telephone
   poles disappear one by one ahead of you.
4. **The road into the water.** The centre line runs under the surface, roofs show beneath it,
   and you choose: east to the crest or west to the chalice.
5. **The bottom of the chalice.** A circle of sky 25 m up, silhouettes at the lip, a flare
   falling, then 350 m of tunnel down to a disc of daylight.

## Look
Late golden hour (the existing `golden` LOOK), hot, steady west wind. Dead-gold grass and dark
blue oaks; the bone-white ring; red-brown crust; grey glossy silt; olive-brown water; pale
concrete and bleached brick. The low west sun means the chalice route walks into the glare.
Sound: wind, the sprinklers behind you, crust cracking, shell crunch, the barge pumps, wires
singing on the pylons, gulls and ravens, frogs at the margin, the powerhouse hum.

## Production and browser cost
**Cheap:** the bowl is mostly flat (8 m terrain grid, 2 m only on paths, terraces and cliffs).
The bathtub ring is a height-driven tint in the terrain shader, so it needs no assets. Crust,
silt and shells are numpy tileable maps (Voronoi cracks) in `lib/textures.py`. Water is one plane
with the existing shader, and dust is fog. Clutter is instanced (stumps, orchard trunks, fence
posts, poles, headstones, pipe segments, willow/tamarisk, rocks: ~6k instances from ~12 meshes).
Lighting uses the existing outdoor vertex-colour bake.

**Hero assets** (`blender/assets/res_*.py`, ~20k tris in all): the dam, intake towers, the
chalice with its shaft and tunnel, the church, a foundation kit, the bridge, the canopy, the gym,
the marina and houseboat, the barge, the pylons; trucks shared with phase 1.

**Expensive:** *everything is visible from the rim.* There are no rooms, so `RoomCuller` doesn't
apply. It needs instance LODs (impostor cards past 150 m), ~64 terrain chunks with 2-3 LOD
levels, and the town merged into silhouettes past 300 m. Target ≤ 350k triangles and ≤ 120 draw
calls from the knoll, the worst view. Long-range AI sight tests and truck pathing cost CPU. The
south `horizon.py` band needs re-rendering (the bowl and dam replace today's fields), and the
hedge's drive gap becomes a real opening.

## Risks
- **Too open = unfair.** Fixes: the glint, the near-miss, slow rifle fire, and a dust front
  guaranteed on the first crossing. Playtest the time to death on Route A.
- **Lost in the trench or the dust.** The spire, towers and chalice must clear every horizon; the
  pylon line points to the exit.
- **Silt slowness reads as a bug** without visible sinking (the eye drops ~0.15 m) and a sucking
  sound.
- **A water edge without swimming** needs shallow margins, then a visible drop-off.
